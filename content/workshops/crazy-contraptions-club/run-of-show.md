# Run-of-Show — Crazy Contraptions Club

**Total runtime: 90 minutes**
_A minute-by-minute so the parent-organizer knows exactly what's next. No guessing, no dead air._

---

## Before kids arrive (15 min prep, not part of the runtime)

- [ ] Clear a big open floor space. Push tables to the walls — kids build on the floor.
- [ ] Dump all materials onto a central "materials table." Don't pre-sort — kids should rummage.
- [ ] Tape a 3-foot demo strip on the floor to show the starting push.
- [ ] Set a visible timer where everyone can see it (wall clock, phone on tripod, whatever).
- [ ] Put a "question parking lot" sheet on the wall. Kids write questions here when they're stuck — you answer in batches, not one-by-one.

---

## 0:00 — 0:05 · Welcome and frame the challenge

- Kids gather in a circle on the floor.
- **Say this:** "Today we're building chain-reaction machines. One push at the start. Then everything moves on its own. Your job: make the longest, craziest sequence you can."
- Show one tiny demo — a domino hitting a cup, a marble rolling into a cardboard tube. 10 seconds, max.
- **Do not** explain every mechanism. They'll figure it out.

## 0:05 — 0:15 · Materials grab + team formation

- Pairs or trios (not solo, not fours — the dynamic breaks at 4).
- Each team gets 90 seconds at the materials table. Grab what looks useful. No hoarding — they can come back.
- Kids claim a build zone on the floor and mark it with masking tape.

## 0:15 — 0:20 · Sketch it first

- **This is the Mastery Gate #1.** Every team has to sketch their machine on paper before touching materials again.
- Give each kid a pencil and a sheet. The sketch must show: where the push starts, four moving parts, and where it ends.
- Walk the room. Ask one question per team: "What happens right after this?" Don't fix — just ask.

## 0:20 — 0:55 · Build phase (35 min)

- Kids build. You circulate.
- **Your three rules as organizer:**
  1. Only answer questions that are already on the parking lot sheet. Forces them to write it down first — 60% of questions self-resolve when they're written out.
  2. No "help mode." If a team is stuck, point them to another team that's solved a similar problem. Kids teaching kids.
  3. 10-minute warning at 0:45. 2-minute warning at 0:53.

- **Watch for:** teams that build the whole thing and then test it. Encourage micro-testing — test each link as you add it.

## 0:55 — 1:05 · Dress-rehearsal runs

- Each team gets three practice runs.
- **Mastery Gate #2:** before their showtime run, a team member must predict out loud what's going to happen — every step, in order.
- If the machine fails mid-run, they get 5 minutes to diagnose and fix. Without adult help.

## 1:05 — 1:25 · Show-and-tell runs

- Each team performs their run for the whole group.
- **Mastery Gate #3:** the kid who pushes the starting piece can only touch **one thing**. If two hands touch two pieces, the run doesn't count — reset and try again.
- Everyone else films on a phone if available. Chapter shares the best clips later.

## 1:25 — 1:30 · Debrief in a circle

- Back on the floor. Two questions, go around:
  1. What's one thing your team figured out that didn't work the first time?
  2. What's one thing you'd add if you had another hour?
- Parent-organizer closes: "You made physics happen with your hands today. That's the whole point."

## Common failures and fast fixes

- **"Our marble keeps going the wrong way"** → Angle the ramp steeper. If the track is flat, marbles wander.
- **"The dominos are too close and they jam"** → Space them at about two-thirds of one domino's height. No closer.
- **"The rubber band snapped"** → Good. That's physics. Ask what it means for the tension they need.
- **"Team X is finished and we're not"** → They're not. Walk them over. Team X's machine has bugs. Everyone's does.

## Clean-up (5 min, off the clock)

- Everyone helps. No exceptions. Kids break down their machines — then we go.
