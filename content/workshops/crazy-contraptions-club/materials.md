# Materials — Crazy Contraptions Club

**Group size:** 8–12 kids, working in pairs or trios
**Estimated cost:** $25–35 total, most of it reusable
**Sourcing time:** 15 minutes at any hardware/craft store, or from your recycling bin

---

## The essentials

- [ ] **Cardboard tubes — a LOT.** Paper towel rolls, toilet paper rolls, mailing tubes. Aim for at least 2 per kid. Start collecting the week before from families in the chapter.
- [ ] **Marbles — at least 40.** Mixed sizes. Small bag at any toy store or Amazon.
- [ ] **Masking tape — 4 rolls.** Don't skimp on tape. This is the #1 material kids run out of.
- [ ] **Rubber bands — assorted pack.** Thick and thin. ~50 count.
- [ ] **Dominos — one full set (or two).** Cheap wooden set is fine. Don't buy the fancy tumbling kind.
- [ ] **String or twine — one spool.**

## Nice-to-haves (grab if you can)

- [ ] Paper cups (small ones, for catches)
- [ ] Plastic spoons (great levers)
- [ ] Wooden skewers (axles for spinning parts)
- [ ] Small toy cars (pre-made rolling pieces)
- [ ] A few marbles of different weights (steel ball bearings mixed in — makes kids notice mass)

## From your recycling bin — free and great

- [ ] Empty cereal boxes (cut into ramps and walls)
- [ ] Plastic bottle caps (wheels, catches)
- [ ] Old magazines (heavy pages = good weights)

## Tools (stay with the organizer)

- [ ] 2–3 pairs of kid-safe scissors
- [ ] 1 roll of thicker duct tape — **only for you**, for emergency structural saves
- [ ] Sharpies — 1 fine, 1 thick
- [ ] A clipboard with the Mastery sign-off chart
- [ ] Phone or stopwatch for timing
- [ ] 2 blank sheets of paper per kid (for sketches)

## What NOT to bring

- Glue. It takes too long to dry. Tape is the entire system.
- Batteries or motors. This workshop is about kinetic energy from one push — adding power defeats the point.
- Pre-built kits. The whole magic is rummaging through random stuff.
- Glitter. Just. No.

---

## Setup tip

Dump everything onto one big "materials table" — unsorted. Kids rummaging is part of the workshop. A pre-sorted table feels like a classroom; a pile feels like a playground.
