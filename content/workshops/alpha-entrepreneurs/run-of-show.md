# Run-of-Show — Alpha Entrepreneurs

**Total runtime: 90 minutes**

---

## Before kids arrive (10 min prep)

- [ ] Tables in pods of 3–4. Each pod = a "company."
- [ ] Stack of poster boards on a side table.
- [ ] Play money pre-sorted in envelopes — each company gets $1,000 in fake bills.
- [ ] One example product on a table: a bad product (a rock glued to a stick, labeled "The Stick Rock — $29.99"). This is your teaching tool.

## 0:00 — 0:10 · The bad pitch

- You, the organizer, pitch "The Stick Rock" to the kids. 60 seconds. Fully committed. "It's a rock. It's on a stick. It solves the problem of rocks not being on sticks. $29.99."
- Ask: **"Would you buy it? Why not?"**
- Let them roast. The lesson writes itself: you need a customer, a problem, and a price that makes sense.

## 0:10 — 0:20 · Form companies + pick a problem

- Teams of 3–4. Each team has **5 minutes** to pick one *real* problem a kid their age has. Not "world peace." Something like "my backpack is too heavy" or "my little brother eats my snacks."
- Each team writes the problem on the top of their poster board.
- **Mastery Gate #1:** the problem has to name a specific customer. "Kids" doesn't count. "11-year-olds who walk to school" does.

## 0:20 — 0:40 · Product + money math

- Each team designs their product (solution to the problem). Sketch it on the poster. Give it a name.
- **The money rule:** the team has $100 of fake budget. They must list:
  - **Cost to make one unit** (material + time, priced realistically)
  - **Price they'll sell it for**
  - **Profit per unit**
- **Mastery Gate #2:** the price can't just be a vibe. They have to explain it. "We charge $15 because it costs us $6 and we want $9 profit" is a great answer. "We charge $100 because we're fancy" is not.

## 0:40 — 1:00 · Build the pitch

- Each team writes a 90-second pitch.
- Pitch must hit 4 beats:
  1. **Who's the customer** (specific person)
  2. **What's the problem** (in their words)
  3. **How does our product solve it** (one sentence)
  4. **Why should you buy it now** (price + the hook)
- Teams practice once with each other. Then once standing up. No scripts during the real pitch.

## 1:00 — 1:20 · Pitch day

- Each team pitches for 90 seconds. Timer visible.
- **You are the judge.** Be warm, but ask one sharp question after each pitch:
  - "How did you get that price?"
  - "Who's a customer that would *not* buy this? Why?"
  - "What's the first thing you'd change if you had another week?"
- **Mastery Gate #3 + #4:** complete pitch + one answered question = mastery.

## 1:20 — 1:30 · Vote and close

- Every kid gets 3 play-money bills. They invest them in any team **other than their own**. They can spread bills or put all 3 on one.
- Count. Winner gets applause. Losers get a "what one customer would you talk to next week?" question.

## Common failures and fast fixes

- **"Our product is an app."** → Too easy, too vague. Force them to specify: what screen, what button, what moment. Or redirect to a physical object.
- **"Everyone priced theirs at $999."** → Kids go wild with price first time. Show them what $999 *means*: "That's your mom's whole week of work. Why would she pay that?"
- **"One kid is doing all the talking."** → Rule for next round: every team member says at least one of the four beats. No exceptions.

## Clean-up (5 min, off the clock)

- Posters come down. Play money back in envelopes. Pitch notes go home with the kids — they wrote them, they own them.
