# Materials — Alpha Entrepreneurs

**Group size:** 8–12 kids (2–3 teams of 3–4)
**Estimated cost:** $15–25 total, all reusable
**Sourcing time:** 15 minutes at an office supply / dollar store

---

## The essentials

- [ ] **Poster board — 1 per team.** 22×28 works. Kids sketch their product and their pitch structure on it.
- [ ] **Sharpies, assorted.** Fine and thick. Posters need to read from across the room.
- [ ] **Play money set.** Monopoly money or any dollar-store cash. Each team starts with $1,000 in fake bills.
- [ ] **Sticky notes — 1 pack per team.** For the cost-math breakdown and iterating on the pitch beats.
- [ ] **Clipboards — 1 per team.** Pitch prep happens standing up half the time.

## Nice-to-haves

- [ ] A visible timer (big enough for the whole room to see during the 90-second pitch)
- [ ] A cheap gavel or bell for the "judge's" questions — leans into the format
- [ ] Colored dot stickers for voting (alternative to play money)

## Tools (stay with the organizer)

- [ ] The **Stick Rock** — your bad-example product. Glue a rock to a stick. Label it dramatically. ($29.99.)
- [ ] Extra play money (teams inevitably lose some)
- [ ] Clipboard with Mastery sign-off chart
- [ ] A printed "judge's question bank" so you've got sharp follow-ups ready for each pitch

## What NOT to bring

- Real money. Obvious. Also: the whole point is that money is pretend so risk is fake and thinking is free.
- Fancy pitch-deck software or laptops. The pitch is verbal + poster. Slides slow down this age group.
- Complicated product kits. This workshop is about thinking, not making. If kids want to *build* the product, that's a different workshop (Experimentation Station).

---

## Setup tip

Pods matter. Round tables with 3–4 kids each beat rows or one big conference table. Each pod should feel like its own company from the moment the kids walk in — put a poster board and an envelope of play money on each pod before kids arrive. Territory = ownership.

## The one prep thing you can't skip

Make the Stick Rock the night before. Commit to the bit. When you pitch it with a straight face, the lesson lands in 30 seconds and every kid gets what bad pitches sound like. Skip it and you're teaching theory.
