# Materials — ASI: Alpha Science Investigators

**Group size:** 8–12 kids (4 teams of 2–3)
**Estimated cost:** $20–30 total, mostly reusable
**Sourcing time:** 30 minutes — longest prep workshop in the catalog

---

## The essentials

- [ ] **Magnifying glasses — 1 per team.** Dollar store kids' magnifiers are perfect.
- [ ] **Ziplock evidence bags — 10+.** Quart size. Pre-label with A, B, C…
- [ ] **Fingerprint ink pad (washable) + white paper.** Only if you're doing the optional print-lifting section.
- [ ] **Printed case files (1 packet per team).** You write these. Suspect dossiers + scene photos + evidence logs. ~8 pages per team.
- [ ] **Latex-free gloves.** Optional but kids love them. It's half the experience.
- [ ] **Badges or printed ID cards** — blank, kids write their own name + title ("Investigator").

## Staged scene materials (one-time setup)

- [ ] A knocked-over chair (just pick one from the room)
- [ ] A handwritten "note" with a partial signature — you write this
- [ ] A disposable coffee cup — lipstick smudge made with a red marker is fine
- [ ] **Flour + a shoe** — press the shoe into flour on a paper to make a "footprint"
- [ ] A random key (house key, bike lock key — anything)
- [ ] A small torn piece of fabric (cut from an old t-shirt)
- [ ] String or masking tape for the "crime scene" perimeter

## Nice-to-haves

- [ ] Clipboards — teams take notes on the move
- [ ] A toy stopwatch or timer (for dramatic effect during the countdown)
- [ ] Red rope or police-tape style ribbon (available from party stores)

## Tools (stay with the organizer)

- [ ] The **master solution sheet** — the actual story of what happened. This is your secret.
- [ ] Printed copies of suspect dossiers (extras, in case teams tear theirs up)
- [ ] Clipboard with Mastery sign-off chart
- [ ] A stopwatch for timing the scene walk

## What NOT to bring

- Real police scanners, real chemicals, or real "CSI" kits. We're playing detective, not doing lab work.
- A solution the kids can't reach from the evidence. The case must be **solvable** from what's on the evidence table.
- Scary themes. It's a missing trophy or a stolen cookie, never a person.

---

## Setup tip

Pick a theme that feels low-stakes but specific: "Who moved the Principal's stapler?" or "Who ate the chapter's birthday cookie?" The higher the silly factor in the *crime*, the more serious kids take the *investigation*. It's magic.

## The one prep thing you can't skip

**Write the solution before the workshop starts.** You need to know exactly what happened and why each piece of evidence fits. If you go in without the answer, the workshop falls apart. Plan on 30 focused minutes of writing the day before.
